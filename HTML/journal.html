FROM THE EDITOR

In praise of
going nowhere

We spend so much time asking where to go next that we forget to ask what a place is doing to us. This issue is an argument for staying a little longer.

It is about the second coffee, the walk you take without a destination, and the conversation that begins after the plates have been cleared. Travel, at its best, is not a list of arrivals. It is a change in attention.

THE CONVERSATION
“A house should remember who walked through it.”

Marco on finding homes with a memory, the sound of a good kitchen, and why he never trusts a perfect photograph.

It is never one thing. It is the worn edge of a table, a kitchen that still smells of the last meal, and the sense that someone has left the windows open on purpose. A house feels lived in when it has been allowed to keep its habits.

Silence is not the absence of sound. It is the moment the house stops asking you to do something—no clock, no screen, no corridor telling you where to go. I know a place has enough when I can hear the garden.

The kitchen, always. Not for the appliances, but for the way people gather there without being asked. If the kitchen is generous, the rest of the house usually follows.

A RECIPE FOR STAYING

Nothing to do,
nowhere to be

The Sunday table is less a meal than a decision. Here is how we set ours.

  1. 01
    Start with the market

    Buy what looks good, not what the recipe asks for. Let the table begin there.

  2. 02
    Set one long table

    Enough room for elbows, serving dishes, and someone who arrives late.

  3. 03
    Forget the clock

    Clear the plates when the light changes. Not before.

Best served with a local wine and no plans after.
